1. WHY YOU, AND WHAT THIS ORDER IS
================================================================
Taskbar Hero ("Lantern Cat Café") is a cozy browser card RPG for the Avalanche
hackathon (submission 21 Nov 2026). Battles render with Three.js 0.180 in an
orthographic camera ("taskbar-cutout" renderer). The owner's own score of the
visuals is about 5/10: the three cats are fine (designer Live2D-style idles),
but the MONSTERS look weak. They are flat painted cut-outs. The code itself
says so: apps/web/src/game/cardrpg/spine/enemy-life.ts opens with "Painted
enemy cut-outs read as stickers".

Every enemy already ships a depth map that the runtime never uses:
apps/web/public/units/<enemy>/rig-depth.webp (also .png / .half.webp) exists
for needle-moth, teacup-healer, button-guard, spool-ram, sock-skipper,
binding-thread, crumb-sentinel, pollen-scout, practice-cushion, kettle-knight,
and the three bosses yarn-giant, rain-spool, pantry-crown. Today enemies get only
a flat saturation/contrast grade (ENEMY_GRADE / ENRAGED_GRADE in
enemy-life.ts, applied through paintGrade() in spine/scene.ts around line 332)
and a small idle sway.

You were picked for this because your measured strength is 3D and visual
judgement. THE GOAL: make every monster and boss read as a lit, solid, grounded
creature standing in the same night-lantern light as the arena, without
touching the cats, without changing any game rule, and without slowing the
frame. Ship it as one draft PR that another agent will merge after review.

5. WHAT TO BUILD
================================================================
PHASE A (must ship): "lit and grounded"
For every enemy and boss, on whichever render path it uses:
A1. Normals. Build a per-pixel normal for the painted cut-out. Preferred:
    from rig-depth where LEARN.md proved it ALIGNED (Sobel on depth, smoothed);
    fallback for MISALIGNED/UNKNOWN: a bevel normal from the alpha channel
    (distance-to-edge, so the silhouette rounds off like a soft toy). Both
    may be computed in the shader from texture samples or baked offline into
    a derived map by a committed script; choose by measured cost and quality.
A2. Lighting model (stylised, not PBR; the art is painterly and must stay so):
    - key light: warm lantern from upper-left/front, colour taken from the
      arena mood (see A5); gentle wrap (half-Lambert), never black shadows;
    - rim light: thin, cool back-rim on the silhouette edge away from the key,
      stronger on bosses, strictly inside the alpha (no halo outside);
    - ambient occlusion at the base and in creases (depth/alpha cavity), so
      the feet sit in shade;
    - keep the painted albedo: the final colour must stay within a small
      luminance band of the original on the lit side (no washed-out, no
      plastic sheen, no specular hotspots except an optional tiny gloss on
      materials that are obviously shiny: teacup, kettle, crown metal).
A3. Grounding: a soft contact shadow under each enemy, offset away from the
    key light, plus a short cast shadow for bosses. Check that it does not
    double up with the existing ground shadow mesh (scene.ts "shadow") or
    PaintLight.ground; replace or tune, do not stack.
A4. Readability rules: enemies must stay readable against every arena; the
    existing ENEMY_GRADE / ENRAGED_GRADE pop must survive; boss phase 2 must
    still read as "hotter and harder" at a glance; hurt flash (hitColor) and
    death (PaintDeath) must still work on top of your shader.
A5. Arena coupling: enemies stand in a chapter mood. On main the arena is the
    garden at night; a pending PR adds moods garden / rain / pantry and boss
    moods tangle / storm / crown via arenaMood(chapter, boss, bossAsset) in
    spine/stage-painting.ts. If that function exists on origin/main when you
    start or after a merge, import it read-only. If not, define your own
    ENEMY_LIGHT_BY_MOOD table in enemy-relight.ts with the same six mood keys
    and pick the mood from chapter + boss asset yourself. Suggested keys:
    garden warm honey key + cool violet rim; rain cool blue-grey key + silver
    rim; pantry amber key + deep brown ambient; tangle magenta-violet; storm
    steel-blue; crown gold. Tune by eye with screenshots.
A6. Kill switch and safety: URL ?enemyLight=0 turns the whole feature off
    (render must then match current main exactly). Respect reduced motion
    (lighting is static; only phase-B animation stops). Deterministic: no
    Math.random, no wall-clock; any animation uses the scene's own time.
    If a WebGL feature (derivatives, float textures) is unavailable, fall back
    to the current flat look, never to a broken one.

PHASE B (ship if A is green): "alive under light"
B1. Directional hit light: when an enemy is hit, a brief light pulse comes
    from the attacker's side (use the normal map, attacker colour from
    hitColor), 120-180 ms, instead of a flat tint flash.
B2. Breathing light: tie a very small key-light modulation to the existing
    enemyIdleLife breath so bodies feel volumetric, not sticker-wobbly.
B3. Boss arrival and phase change: a rim flare sweep on landing
    (boss-arrival.ts) and a stronger hot rim in phase 2 (boss-phase.ts).

PHASE C (stretch, only if A and B are green and time remains before freeze)
C1. Death "unravel": enemies come apart along a depth/noise threshold with a
    yarn-coloured glowing edge, compatible with PaintDeath (extend it through
    a new vfx-craft/enemy-*.ts file; do not rewrite paint-death.ts).
C2. A gallery capture of all 13 enemies lit in all moods (for the deck and
    the trailer team) at docs/astra-enemy-light-2026-11-21/gallery/.
